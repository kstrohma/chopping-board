How the chop % is figured

  1. Start with the projections. Every starter has a projected score — the points Fleaflicker expects them to put up this week.
  2. Nobody hits their projection exactly. So instead of one number we give each player a realistic range of outcomes — wide and boom-or-bust for a WR or TE, tighter for a QB — and let teammates who play together rise and fall as a group.
  3. Play the week out 50,000 times. In each run every player draws a score from their range and we total up each team — 50,000 different ways the same week could realistically go.
  4. Count who finishes last. In each run the lowest-scoring team gets chopped. A team's chop % is just how often it came out last across all 50,000 runs.

So a 20% means: in about 1 out of every 5 ways the week could plausibly play out, this team posts the league low and loses its head. A lower projection and a shakier lineup both push the number up.

The bar on each row is that team's likely score range — 10th to 90th percentile of the runs, with the median tick. Bottom-2 is how often a team lands in the lowest two. The spread we use per position is an educated estimate, not tuned to this league yet, so read close margins as a guide rather than gospel. During games, a player is credited with the points banked so far plus part of what's still to come.
